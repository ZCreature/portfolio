About

I learned structure in print and put it to work on data.

I started in print production — signage, wide-format, colour management. The unglamorous end, where a mistake costs material. It taught me specifications before it taught me taste.

Then seven years of advertising, then a hard turn into editorial. Four years of news graphics for California business journals — strict grids, standing type, daily deadlines. A chart needing three paragraphs of explanation has failed, and a newsroom tells you immediately.

Now I build data products for U.S. hospital systems, read by executives making decisions with real consequences. The constraints changed; the discipline didn't. Structure first, then clarity, then — if there's room — elegance.

Lately I build my own tools when the ones I need don't exist. I'm a designer, not an engineer, and I'm careful about the difference — but knowing how a thing is made has changed how I design it.
