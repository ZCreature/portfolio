Case study — 07 / Healthcare

Surgical dressing utilization

Six Sankey studies tracing surgical cases from the dressing used, through patient type, to whether a complication was recorded.

Role

Design

Year

2022

Team

Vizient — Data Intelligence & Visualization

Output

Presentation slides

01 — Context

Whether advanced dressings change what happens after surgery

Two categories of advanced wound dressing were in question: silver dressings on orthopedic cases, and negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT) kits on cardiac and skin cases. Both cost more than a standard dressing. The analysis asked how often each was used, and whether cases that used one recorded fewer complications than cases that did not.

The underlying table was a case-level extract with four usable columns: the dressing category, whether the case was inpatient or outpatient, whether a complication was flagged, and which complication codes applied. Complication data existed for inpatient cases only.

02 — Process

Three versions of the knee chart before the pattern was fixed

Total knee replacement was drawn first and used to settle the format. The first version, shown large below, squared every flow into blocks and put the cohort total in a circle at the left edge. It exposed the problem that shaped everything after it: 20,333 of the cases were outpatient and carried no complication data, so the largest rectangle on the slide was also the one with the least to say.

The second version replaced the blocks with curved bands and dropped the circle. Following an individual flow got easier, but the space recovered went into the written insight in the left column rather than into the diagram, which left the reading dependent on the prose beside it.

The third version kept the curves, filled the outpatient block with diagonal hatching labelled in place, and ranked the complication codes down the right-hand edge instead of leaving them as undifferentiated endpoints. That version became the template. Hip followed it without change. CABG and skin diverged where the data required it, so the four procedure families could still be read against each other without relearning the diagram.

03 — Design

Two hues, and everything else carried by structure

Colour encodes one variable only. Teal marks cases with no dressing and purple marks cases with a dressing or kit, and that pairing holds across all six slides. Later stages use lighter tints of whichever hue the case started in, so an arm can be followed from left to right without a legend.

Missing data is drawn rather than omitted. Outpatient cases had no complication records, so those blocks are filled with diagonal hatching and labelled in place. The alternative, dropping them, would have made the inpatient share of each cohort look larger than it was.

Small values were the recurring problem. A count of 3 against a cohort of 9,075 has no drawable thickness, so those flows leave the diagram as thin curves and terminate in a figure and a label at full size. The CABG slide is the clearest case: 9,075 cases used no dressing against 2,049 using negative pressure kits, and the complication counts it resolves to are 37, 535, 3 and 133.

The last stage changes according to what was being asked. Knee and hip rank the ten most frequent complication codes for each arm. CABG splits wound-related from non-wound-related complications and stops there. The skin procedures run to five stages, inserting that wound split before the code list so the two questions resolve in sequence rather than collapsing into one column. The source line on every slide reads XXXXXXXX, as the data source was withheld from the deck.
