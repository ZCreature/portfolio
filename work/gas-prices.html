Case study — 04 / Exploratory

Prompting is not designing

An interactive study of North American gas prices, built in R with Leaflet. I wrote it with AI assistance, which got me to a working map fast — and then cost me a long evening of specifying exactly what "looks right" means.

02 — What it took

Not a specification

The first working version arrived quickly and looked like every default R map: rainbow bins, a legend in the wrong corner, state boundaries competing with the data for attention.

Getting past that meant writing down things I normally decide by eye — bin breaks that follow the distribution rather than equal intervals, a sequential ramp instead of a categorical one, boundaries dropped back to a hairline, labels only where they don't collide.

Every prompt that produced something worth keeping was a prompt where I'd already made the design decision.

That's the honest finding, and it's the same one from the Color Safety Lens project from a different angle. AI-assisted development collapses the distance between an idea and something that runs. It does nothing for the distance between something that runs and something that's right — that gap is still the job.
