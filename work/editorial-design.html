Editorial page design

Covers, feature spreads and pages in print for the Silicon Valley, Sacramento and San Francisco business journals. The same grid that carried the weekly data page, carrying the rest of the paper.

ACBJ Print

01 - Context

A cover is read at three distances

On a rack, a reader gets a second and sees one shape and one headline. In the hand, they read the deck and the chart. On a phone it is a thumbnail, and everything but the largest element disappears.

Which distance mattered was never mine to choose, so the page had to hold all three: one dominant element, a headline that survives being shrunk, and a palette tight enough to read beside eleven others in a feed.

02 - News pages

The pages in between

Between the weekly data page and the cover story sat most of the paper: local news, one story at a time, usually one chart or one illustration and whatever space the section had left that week. Less room than a cover and the same deadline.

03 - Impact

The charts had to survive the cover

The part I would defend is that the data did not get demoted to make room for the art. A darkening market is a photograph and a falling red line on the same page. A funding gap is a campus photograph, one large number, and a bar chart of enrollment. The chart is not an illustration parked next to the story, it is the reason the headline is allowed to make its claim.

Keeping both legible is a constraint problem more than a taste problem. Two colors and one dominant image leave enough contrast for a chart to hold its own at thumbnail size; four colors and a busy photograph do not, and the chart is always what gets sacrificed.

Four years of this, on a weekly cycle, across three mastheads. The same rules carried a routine market wrap and a breaking cover story without being renegotiated each time, which is the only real test of whether a set of rules is a system or a preference.
