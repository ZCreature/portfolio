Side project - Editorial data visualization

Financial opportunity, by size and by speed

An executive team asking where to spend its attention has two questions, and they fight each other. Which opportunity is biggest, and which one pays out soonest. This is one picture that answers both.

Bubble chart Editorial Healthcare

01 - The problem

A table makes you do the work

The source for this was a table: a row per category, a dollar column, and a column for how many months until the money shows up. Everything needed was in it, and nobody could see the answer. Ranking by dollars buries the quick wins. Ranking by time buries the big ones. A reader has to hold both columns in their head and do the comparison themselves, which is exactly the work a chart is supposed to take off them.

The decision this had to support was not "which number is largest." It was where a leadership team should point its next twelve months.

02 - The encoding

Three channels, one for each thing being asked

Area is the money

Circles scaled by dollar opportunity. Area is a weak channel for precise comparison, which is correct here: nobody should read exact dollars off a bubble, and the figures are printed anyway.

Distance is the wait

Position along the arc is time to value, from three months at the center out past eighteen. Distance from the origin is the delay, so far away literally means far off.

Color is the kind of work

Two categories only, clinical operations against spend management, which stays well under the point where color stops separating reliably.

The fan is the effort

The grey wedges widen with the work required to reach each one, so the shape carries a third variable without adding a legend nobody reads.

03 - Why an arc

The shape answers before the labels do

A scatter plot would have carried the same two variables and read as homework. The arc does one thing a grid cannot: it puts the origin where the reader is standing. Near is soon, far is later, and the largest circle sitting furthest out is the prize that costs the most patience.

That is the sentence the room says out loud when this goes up, which is the only real test of a chart built for a meeting.

04 - The other cut

Same data, scale made explicit

A second version trades the arc for a straight month scale across the top. It is easier to read a specific number off and much harder to see the story in, which is the trade you make every time you turn a picture back into a table.
